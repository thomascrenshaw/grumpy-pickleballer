🥒 Talk to Your Partner

One word, early, loud. The calls that keep two people from both going for the same ball, or neither.

Plenty of points get lost because both players went for the ball, or neither did. The fix is a handful of one-word calls you both know before the game starts.

The calls

SayIt meansWhen
Mine!I'm taking it.Any ball either of you could reach. Usually, whoever calls first gets it.
I got it / I goSame as Mine. Redundant, but common.Use whichever comes out of your mouth fastest.
Yours! / Go!You take it.When your partner has the better angle or the forehand.
Switch!We're trading sides.After a lob over one player, or when one of you gets pulled wide.
Stay!Don't switch. I've got it covered.When a switch would leave more open court than it closes.
I got your sideI'm covering the space you left.When your partner is out of position and can't get back yet.
No! / Out!Don't hit it. It's going out.A ball headed long or wide. Say it before your partner swings.
Bounce!Let it bounce. It might be out.A deep ball you're not sure about. If it lands in, you can still play it.

How to use them

  1. One word.There isn't time for a sentence. “Mine” beats “I think I can get that one.”
  2. Early.Call it as the ball leaves their paddle, not as it arrives at yours.
  3. Loud.Your partner is watching the ball, not you. They need to hear it.
  4. Agree before you start.New partner? Take ten seconds before the first serve: “What do you say for switch?”
When nobody calls it: the player with the forehand in the middle usually takes it. That's the default from Positioning Basics, and it settles a lot of the balls that come down the center.

For the calls that matter most, a lob over your head or getting pulled wide, see Who Gets the Lob.
