🥒 Line Calls

In is the default. Out takes certainty. It's only a point.

If you think it was out, it was in.

If you're not sure, it's in

If you aren't sure a ball is out, it's in. If you think it was out, it was in. In is the default, unless you're sure it's out. No discussion.

If you and your partner can't agree, it was in.

Call it the best you can

It's only a point. Call it the best you can, given that you were probably chasing the ball and it was moving fast.

Who has time to look down at the line and process where it landed when you're trying not to hit the fence, chasing a lob that sailed over your partner's head, after looking into the sun just long enough that the only thing you can still see is the ball?

Why I call it out

If I hit the ball back over the net and then call it out, it's because my brain is old and slow, not because I want an advantage. If I can hit it, why would I call it out if it was in? I just want to play. But out is out.

Sure, I make mistakes, just like everyone else. We're playing for fun, for exercise, for the camaraderie, and because we love this ducking game.

Don't play line judge from 29 or 44 feet away

The other side's lines are theirs to call. Don't play line judge from your kitchen line (29 feet from their baseline) or your own baseline (44 feet).4 Just move on to the next point.

Nobody argues an in call

Plenty of people will argue an out call they felt was in, even when nobody could see the line because the net, your partner, or a leaf was in the way.

The rulebook agrees. You call the lines on your side of the net.1 A ball you can't promptly call out is in.2 If you and your partner disagree, it's in.3

There will always be contested line calls. How you handle them says a lot about what kind of player you are, and how you approach the crew or the rec game. Tournaments are different. This is regular ole playing-with-my-friends pickleball.

An out call comes from one of three places:

  1. The ball was out. Out is always out.
  2. The ball was in, and your opponent made a mistake.
  3. The ball was in, and your opponent really wants the point, the second serve, or the side out.

Whichever one it is, a single call isn't worth the pickleball time you lose arguing about it. The exception is a repeat offender, and an egregious one.

The calls themselves, Out and Bounce included, are on Talk to Your Partner.

Sources

  1. USA Pickleball, 2026 Official Rulebook, Rule 8.A: players make the "out" calls for the lines on their end of the court. ↩
  2. USA Pickleball, 2026 Official Rulebook, Rule 8.D: any ball that can't be promptly called "out" must be considered "in." ↩
  3. USA Pickleball, 2026 Official Rulebook, Rule 8.H: when partners disagree on a line call, the team's call is "in." ↩
  4. USA Pickleball, 2026 Official Rulebook, Rules 3.A.1 and 3.A.4.c: the court is 44 feet long and the non-volley zone runs 7 feet from the net, so it's 22 feet from the net to each baseline. ↩
