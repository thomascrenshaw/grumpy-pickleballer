🥒 The Pre-Snap Read

Returning serve like a quarterback reads a defense. Everything I'm looking at before the ball gets to me. This is my routine, about a year in; yours might look nothing like it.

Watching quarterback shows has dialed in the way I'm starting to look at pickleball. Before the snap, a quarterback reads the defense. Before the serve, I read the court.

When I'm the receiver, I'm not just waiting for the ball. I'm running a checklist, and most of it happens before the serve is even struck. It sounds like a lot. It is a lot. But brains are pretty fast.

The read, in order

  1. The formation.Where is the serve coming from? Where is the server's partner standing? How much space is between them?
  2. The snap.Watch the ball as it leaves the paddle. Is it low, high, or somewhere in the middle? That changes everything about what I hit back.
  3. The call.Match the shot to what's coming. The table below is my playbook.
  4. The conditions.Is it windy? How is that going to push the ball, and where does that leave my placement?

The call

What's comingWhat I do with it
Mid-speed, mid-depth, mid-heightPerfect candidate for a slice, especially if either opponent has trouble with slice.
Low and fastReturn it with topspin, so it clears the net and drops.
Backspin on the serveTwo choices. Get under it and just get it over the net. Or get way under it and topspin the hell out of that ball. Watch it float, then drop like a rock.

Reading the coverage

Once the rally starts, the read keeps going. When a player moves over to cover the middle, the first thing that opens up is the down-the-line shot. It's the same thing a quarterback sees when a safety cheats inside: the sideline behind him is suddenly empty.

Most of us don't look for it yet. Around the 2.5 to 3.5 level, almost everyone is thinking cross-court. The players already hitting down the line are usually the ex-tennis players, which is exactly why it works so well for them. Mine land about half the time right now, which puts me a little above the curve.

Know your crew

Playing with a crew is a lot like the pro tour rolling from one event into the next and back again: you keep seeing the same people. That means part of the read is done before the first serve. I already know who cheats a step toward the middle but has fast hands (like me), and who can run down a lob and actually will. The scouting report writes itself if you pay attention.

Then turn it off

All of this reading happens before the ball arrives. Once the point is over, it has to stop, or I'm still solving the last rally while the next one starts. That's what the ritual is for: read before the snap, quiet after the whistle.

Brains are pretty fast. The only one I know really well is pretty fast on some things.
