How it decides. Each round, whoever has sat out the least sits first, and nobody sits twice in a row if it can be avoided. Then it tries a pile of shuffles and keeps the one with the fewest repeat partners. Each round has its own Copy button, so you can post rounds to the group chat one at a time instead of yelling them across the courts. Rotating partners also spreads out who you play against. Fixed partners uses the standard circle rotation, so every team sees every other team before anyone repeats. Mid-session, tap Start on the round you're playing: everything up to it is locked. Mark someone Out (late, leaving, or sitting a stretch) or tap Back, and only the rounds after that get rebuilt, with sit-outs counted only for the rounds each person was there. Previous shuffle brings back the schedule you had before the last reshuffle. A partner request locks one pair together for the games you set (and only those), picks their opponents from your list, and builds everyone else's rounds around them. Want their game in a different round? Swap them together there by hand, and their nearest other game goes back to normal. Split them by hand and that round stops being theirs. Skill balance is a tiebreaker on top of all that, except for its two rules, which come first: a newer pair never faces a Strong player, and two Strong players don't partner if it can be avoided. Keeping two Strong players apart can cost a repeat partner or two; that's the math, not a bug.

How to run a pickleball round robin

A round robin mixes everyone who showed up across your courts, round after round, so nobody plays the same three people all session and the sit-outs are shared. Pick a format, set players, courts and rounds, and the generator does the rest.

Which format?

  • Popcorn: random partners every round. Quick and social, and the default for most drop-in play.
  • Rotating partners (a mixer): it works harder to spread out partners and opponents, so you play with and against as many people as possible.
  • Fixed partners: teams stay together and opponents rotate in a circle, so every team sees every other team before anyone repeats. Good for practicing with your tournament partner.

Odd numbers and sit-outs

Each court takes four players. Everyone else sits out that round, and the generator rotates who sits so it evens out. Nine players on two courts means one sits out each round; eleven means three.

Getting the schedule to everyone

Use Copy for the group chat for the whole schedule, or the Copy button on each round to post them one at a time. Nobody has to yell court assignments across the fence.

Questions

Is this round robin generator free?

Yes. No ads, no account, no app to install. It runs in your browser.

Does it work on a phone at the court?

Yes, it's built for phones. Add it to your home screen and it opens like an app. Your roster and schedule survive a refresh.

Is this a Pickleheads alternative?

For round robins, yes. If you mostly use Pickleheads to generate round robins, this does that part for free in your browser. Pickleheads does much more, like finding games, running sessions and groups. This only does the round robin.

What happens with an odd number of players?

Whoever has sat out the least sits first, and nobody sits twice in a row if it can be avoided. With fixed partners and an odd count, the extra player is the sub.

How many rounds should I schedule?

With rotating partners, a multiple of 4 players and every court full, the number of players minus one is enough for everyone to partner everyone once. Otherwise, schedule as many rounds as your court time allows.

Where are player names stored?

Only in your browser, on your device. Nothing is sent to this site or anywhere else.
