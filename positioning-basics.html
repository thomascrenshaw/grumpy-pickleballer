🥒 Positioning Basics

Six fundamentals for doubles play as you progress. Net at top, kitchen shaded. You & partner dark, opponents amber, dashed = move, orange = ball.

You (Y) & partner (P) Opponents → ball

1 · Get to the Kitchen — Together

The goal is the NVZ line, together. The receiving team starts on offense and the serving team starts on defense, just because of where each side begins. Two up beats one-up-one-back. More below.

2 · Move as a Team — the Rope

A 10-ft rope ties you to your partner. Ball goes left, both slide left. Cover the court as a unit; don't let the rope snap.

3 · The Middle — Usually the Forehand

The simplest way to cover the middle, especially with a partner you don't know, is to let the forehand take it. Talk about it with your partner before you start. It isn't the only way to play the middle, but in a fast hands battle there's no time to talk: the kitchen lines are 14 feet apart, and a hard ball covers that in about a quarter of a second.

4 · Stacking (basics)

Line up same-side pre-serve, cross after. Keeps both forehands in the middle or hides a weak backhand.

Deep dive →

5 · Wide Ball — Partner Plugs the Gap

One of you gets pulled wide; the other slides to the middle to cover the open lane. The rope, applied to recovery.

6 · Reset — the Attrition Shot

Out of position? Don't win it — drop a soft ball in their kitchen and reset. Get it over one more time; let them miss.

Why the serving team starts on defense

It comes down to starting positions. After the return, the receiving team has one player already at the kitchen line and the other on the way. The serving team starts with both players back at the baseline and has to earn its way forward.

So the serving team should make getting to the NVZ line a priority, but not at the expense of leaving gaps for the other team to put balls through. The line is the goal, and you want to get there as soon as you can. What you don't want is one of you up at the line while the other is still stuck at the baseline.

One up, one back: the gap between you is the other team's easiest target. Newer players end up here a lot, and so does a receiving partner who's hesitant to come up to the line.

The fix is patience. The partner who isn't hitting the third shot shouldn't head to the line alone. If you're at the baseline, hit a drop and step forward together. If the drop wasn't your best, drop the next one and move forward again.

Drop, step in together, repeat. You move up as a pair.

Remember where you are. Between the baseline and the kitchen line is the transition zone, and it gives the other team a lot more angles and opportunities. Once the serving team reaches the NVZ line too, the point is neutral again.
