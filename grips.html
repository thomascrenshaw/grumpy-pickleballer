🥒 Grips

Where your hand goes on the handle, and which grip goes with which shot. Three of them show up in a single rally of mine. This is my routine, about a year in; yours might look nothing like it.

The grip decides what the paddle face is doing before you ever swing. Turn your hand one notch on the handle and the same swing hits a different shot.

Find the bevels

Your handle isn't round. It has eight flat sides, called bevels. Hold the paddle with the edge pointing straight up and look at the butt cap: the top flat is bevel 1, and you count clockwise from there (lefties, count counterclockwise). Each grip is named for where the big knuckle at the base of your index finger lands.

The four grips

Moving from continental to western, your hand turns further around the handle and the paddle face closes. A closed face brushes up the back of the ball, which is where topspin comes from. The trade is that the further you go, the harder it is to hit anything else.

What I actually use

The serve: deep western. Palm nearly under the handle, brush up the back of the ball, topspin. The serve is the one shot where you get all the time you want to set your hand, so it gets the most extreme grip I've got.

The kitchen line: continental. Mostly so I can backhand the ball without changing anything. When the dinks speed up, there's no time to turn your hand, and continental doesn't ask you to.

A ball up over my right shoulder: the paddle turns my hand. I let the momentum of the swing rotate the grip around to what I think is western. I don't plan it; the paddle just gets there.

I looked it up, because "what I think is western" isn't a grip. For a true overhead, coaches teach continental: it lets you lead with the edge and turn the paddle over into the ball, and that forearm rotation is where the power comes from. For a high forehand put-away, around shoulder height and swinging forward, shifting toward semi-western or western is legit. Which one mine is, I'm still sorting out.

Leave on purpose, come back every time

Every grip other than your home grip is a trip. You go there for one shot, and then you come back before the next ball. After the serve, that means getting out of western before the return shows up. After a put-away, it means back to continental before the counter.

Know your home grip. Leave it on purpose. Come back every time.
Described for right-handers; lefties, mirror everything. Not coaching, just what's kept my paddle pointed the right way.
